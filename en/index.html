Human–computer interaction

The common thread in my work: understanding human activity (e.g. dental surgery gestures or operations in radioactive environments) to design tools that support learning, decision-making and action. Motion is my main source of information, but I also explore other modalities to assist people.

Motion analysis

Automatic motion analysis is a key strength of my profile and the main expertise I developed during my PhD. From motion traces, I design and integrate gesture and posture analysis tools to address experts’ observation needs.

To support this work, I developed hands-on expertise in motion capture with different systems: marker-based optical capture (e.g. Qualisys), markerless capture (e.g. Azure Kinect) and inertial capture (e.g. Perception Neuron).

Extended reality · XR

I enjoy working with virtual and augmented reality for their ability to present information in 3D and place it in the user’s environment. I also develop desktop, mobile and web interfaces according to users’ needs and contexts.

Unreal Engine is a key strength in my XR development skills. I used it to build my latest virtual environment, VEEGO, and taught it to third-year computer science students.

Guiding principles

I am interested in applied research. Aware of the gap between academic research and its practical applications, I work to overcome technical and methodological barriers and deliver solutions that are practical, scalable, affordable, non-intrusive, easy to deploy in the field and easy for end users to adopt. The traceability of my working methodology is also a major priority.
