Context

During a CBRNe incident (chemical, biological, radiological, nuclear or explosive), first responders must assess hazards that are not always directly perceptible. My internship at LIST explored augmented reality on HoloLens 2 to improve their situation awareness and support risk management, focusing on radiological environments. Sensors carried by unmanned ground vehicles and drones collect measurements and locate hazards. The challenge was to present this information within responders’ field of view.

Contribution

I developed two HoloLens 2 applications: AnchorSpawner to simulate field data and RadareX to display it in an augmented reality interface. Without access to the field, these tools allowed me to design and test information visualisation in a simulated environment.

AnchorSpawner · Simulating data

  • Simulating data transmitted by an unmanned ground vehicle or a remote team.
  • Placing anchors to represent hazardous locations and safe waypoints.
  • Free-space or surface placement, with individual or global anchor deletion.

RadareX · Visualising information

RadareX uses incoming points of interest to make risks visible. A hand-activated menu shows or hides information to reduce clutter in the field of view.

  1. Locate the nearest source

    • Indicating the position and distance of the nearest radioactive source.
    • Displaying its dose rate and the number of identified sources.

  2. Represent cumulative dose

    • Visualising the estimated cumulative dose while moving through the simulated environment.
    • Remaining intervention time represented by a radial gauge, shifting from green to red according to exposure and European thresholds to indicate when to leave the area.

  3. Visualise an exit route

    • Displaying a route based on simulated safe waypoints.
    • Augmented reality markers to guide the exit from the affected area.
